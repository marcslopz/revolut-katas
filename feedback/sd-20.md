## A. Assessment: PASS (technically strong, too slow)

Technically this is one of your best fintech mocks:
- the money path and the saga were right
- the double-entry ledger had a clearing account
- "unique within what?" held twice: `(cell, transfer_id)` and the provider ids
- every number was copied correctly
- Phase 4 was opened by you

**The issue is time: 62 minutes.** In the real round you'd still be in the deep dive when the interviewer wraps up.

## B. Three strongest things

1. **The cross-cell saga**: hold in the source, an idempotent call keyed by `(cell, transfer_id)`, retry rather than
   compensate on a timeout, compensation only on failure, and a ledger with a clearing account per cell.
2. **Probing the provider's contract** in Phase 1. Asking about sync vs async, the delivery guarantee and the status
   API meant the withdrawal design was almost complete from the start.
3. **Correct numbers, all the way through**, the fix you'd been missing for three cases in a row.

## C. Three biggest risks for the real interview

1. **Time.** Requirements in 19 minutes and the deep dive in 34. See H for concrete cuts.
2. **The crash between commit and the external call.** You noted it as an "improvement" (the outbox), but in the
   money path it's part of the minimum: if the process dies after holding and before calling, the money stays held
   forever.
3. **Walking through only one flow in the high level.** The East deposit routing came out only because I probed.
   A 2-minute pass over every flow would have surfaced it.

## D. Architectural gaps or inconsistencies

- The diagram has the provider only on West, while the stated fact was "one endpoint, by IBAN". The gateway and
  lookup came up verbally but aren't drawn.
- `transfers.to_account_id` is described as "not FK since external", but 90% of transfers are within the same cell.
- In a same-cell transfer, `available` is decremented but the sender's `balance` never is.

## E. Missing non-functional considerations

- A metric for **cross-cell transfers stuck in `sent`** (pending past the 10 s p99): that's the saga's health.
- A ledger reconciliation **between cells** (West's clearing account vs East's).

## F. Over/under-engineering

- **Right-sized**: one DB per cell, no replicas, no sharding until the number calls for it.
- **Under**: the outbox for the cross-cell call and the provider call, which belongs in the MVP of a money path.
- **Over (minor)**: synchronous HA replication across regions.

## G. What a strong candidate might have covered

- Draw a **directory service** (IBAN/user → cell, minimal data) between the gateway and both cells.
- **Outbox in the source tx**: hold `available` + insert transfer + outbox row in one transaction. A relay
  delivers it to the destination, which is idempotent on `(source_cell, transfer_id)`. Its ack (or rejection)
  finalizes it (`balance −=`) or compensates (`available +=`).
- **States as a table**:
  - transfer: `created → sent → confirmed | failed`
  - withdrawal: `created → pending → succeeded | failed`, where `created` means held but not yet accepted, and is
    swept by a worker that queries the status API
- A **hot account**, such as a merchant receiving thousands of transfers: credits are commutative, so you can batch
  them or split the account into sub-accounts.

## I. A better architecture (post-mock, brief)

**Boxes:**
- gateway, with a directory (IBAN/user → cell)
- per cell: API + Postgres (multi-AZ hot standby) + an outbox relay + a provider worker

**Ledger per cell:** double entry, with system accounts for the provider (in and out) and cross-cell clearing.

**Flows:**
- **Transfer:**
  - same cell: one tx, locking both accounts in id order
  - cross cell: a saga over the outbox, idempotent on `(source_cell, transfer_id)`
- **Deposit:** idempotent on `provider_transaction_id`, routed by IBAN.
- **Withdrawal:** hold + outbox, then the provider call with `withdrawal_id`. The webhook finalizes or refunds. A
  worker runs status checks for anything stuck.

**Scale:** more cells; shard by user within a cell at about 10x.

## K. Real-world bonus (not scored)

- Real banks run cross-entity transfers through **internal clearing accounts** reconciled daily, which is exactly
  the account you drew.
- **SEPA Instant** gives deposits and withdrawals a 10-second final answer. SEPA credit transfers can come back as
  **R-transactions** (returns) days later, the "failure 2 days later" in this prompt.
- **Safeguarding**: an e-money institution keeps customer funds in segregated accounts, so ledger reconciliation
  against the bank statement is a regulatory requirement, not just hygiene.
